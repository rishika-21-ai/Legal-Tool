# Checklist: NDA / Confidentiality Agreement

Apply **in addition to** `common-clauses.md`. These are the clauses specific to
non-disclosure / confidentiality agreements.

Each entry: **Why it matters** · **Look for** · **Red flags** · **If missing**.

---

## Mutual vs one-way
- **Why it matters:** Decides whether only one side, or both, must protect
  information.
- **Look for:** If both sides share information, it should be **mutual**.
- **Red flags:** A one-way NDA when the user will also disclose its own
  information.
- **If missing:** Convert to mutual, or add reciprocal obligations.

## Definition of Confidential Information
- **Why it matters:** Sets what is actually protected.
- **Look for:** A clear definition covering written, oral, and visual
  information, and whether it must be marked "confidential."
- **Red flags:** So narrow it excludes oral disclosures; or so broad it covers
  everything including public info.
- **If missing:** Add a definition covering non-public business, technical, and
  financial information disclosed in any form.

## Standard exclusions
- **Why it matters:** Information that shouldn't be locked up.
- **Look for:** Carve-outs for info that is public, already known, independently
  developed, or received from a third party without breach.
- **Red flags:** No exclusions — makes the NDA overbroad and hard to comply with.
- **If missing:** Add the four standard exclusions above.

## Permitted use / purpose
- **Why it matters:** Limits what the receiver may do with the information.
- **Look for:** A stated purpose (e.g. "solely to evaluate a potential business
  relationship").
- **Red flags:** No purpose limit; receiver free to use the info for anything.
- **If missing:** Add a purpose clause restricting use to the intended
  relationship.

## Permitted disclosures (need-to-know & legal)
- **Why it matters:** Lets the receiver share with staff/advisors and comply with
  law.
- **Look for:** Disclosure to employees/advisors on a need-to-know basis under
  confidentiality, plus a carve-out for legally compelled disclosure with notice.
- **Red flags:** No legal-compulsion carve-out (impossible to comply with a
  subpoena); no need-to-know sharing.
- **If missing:** Add need-to-know and legally-required-disclosure carve-outs.

## Term of confidentiality
- **Why it matters:** How long the duty to protect lasts.
- **Look for:** A defined period (commonly 2–5 years; indefinite for trade
  secrets).
- **Red flags:** No end date for ordinary business info; or a period so short it
  offers no protection.
- **If missing:** Add a confidentiality period (e.g. 3 years from disclosure).

## Return or destruction of information
- **Why it matters:** Ensures the info is dealt with when the relationship ends.
- **Look for:** Obligation to return or destroy on request, with optional
  archival copies for compliance.
- **Red flags:** No return/destruction duty; receiver may keep everything
  forever.
- **If missing:** Add a return-or-destroy-on-request clause.

## No license / no obligation
- **Why it matters:** Sharing info shouldn't transfer IP rights or force a deal.
- **Look for:** A statement that no license or ownership is granted and no party
  is obligated to proceed with a transaction.
- **Red flags:** Language implying disclosure grants rights to use the underlying
  IP.
- **If missing:** Add "No license or ownership in the disclosed information is
  granted, and nothing obligates either party to enter a further agreement."

## Remedies / injunctive relief
- **Why it matters:** Money damages often can't undo a leak; injunctions can stop
  one.
- **Look for:** Acknowledgement that breach may cause irreparable harm and that
  injunctive relief is available.
- **Red flags:** Only the other party gets this remedy.
- **If missing:** Add a mutual injunctive-relief clause.

## Residuals clause (watch closely)
- **Why it matters:** A "residuals" clause lets the receiver use general
  knowledge retained in memory — it can quietly gut the NDA.
- **Look for:** Whether one exists and how broad it is.
- **Red flags:** A broad residuals clause benefiting the other side; treat as
  High severity for the disclosing party.
- **If present and one-sided:** Recommend narrowing or removing it.
