# Checklist: Common Clauses (most agreements)

Clauses that belong in almost any commercial agreement. Apply this file to
**every** review, in addition to the agreement-type file.

Each entry: **Why it matters** · **Look for** · **Red flags** · **If missing**.

---

## Parties & signatures
- **Why it matters:** The agreement must clearly name who is bound, with correct
  legal entity names, and be signed by authorized people.
- **Look for:** Full legal names/entities, addresses, and a signature block with
  name, title, and date for each party.
- **Red flags:** Wrong or informal entity names; no signature block; one party
  can sign but the other only "acknowledges."
- **If missing:** Add a parties clause with full legal names and a dated
  signature block for each side.

## Term (duration)
- **Why it matters:** Defines when the agreement starts and how long it lasts.
- **Look for:** Clear start date, fixed term or ongoing, and any renewal terms.
- **Red flags:** No end date; perpetual obligations with no exit.
- **If missing:** Add a term clause stating the start date and duration (e.g. "in
  effect for 12 months from the Effective Date").

## Termination
- **Why it matters:** Lets each side exit — for breach, and often for convenience.
- **Look for:** Termination **for cause** (with a cure period), and ideally
  termination **for convenience** with notice; what happens on termination.
- **Red flags:** Only one side can terminate; no cure period; immediate
  termination for any reason by the other party only.
- **If missing:** Add mutual termination rights: for material breach uncured
  after 30 days' notice, and optionally for convenience on 30 days' notice.

## Governing law & jurisdiction
- **Why it matters:** Decides which law applies and where disputes are heard.
- **Look for:** A named governing law and a chosen court/venue.
- **Red flags:** A jurisdiction inconvenient or unfavorable to the user; governing
  law that differs from where the user operates.
- **If missing:** Add "This Agreement is governed by the laws of [state/country],
  and the courts of [location] have exclusive jurisdiction."

## Dispute resolution
- **Why it matters:** Sets how disagreements are resolved (courts, arbitration,
  mediation first).
- **Look for:** A clear path; if arbitration, where and under which rules.
- **Red flags:** Mandatory arbitration in a distant venue; a waiver of the right
  to go to court hidden in fine print.
- **If missing:** Add a clause (e.g. good-faith negotiation, then the chosen
  forum from the governing-law clause).

## Limitation of liability
- **Why it matters:** Caps how much each side can owe if things go wrong — often
  the most financially important clause.
- **Look for:** A **cap** on total liability, and exclusion of indirect/
  consequential damages, applied **mutually**.
- **Red flags:** No cap; a cap that protects only the other side; the user
  carries unlimited liability while the other side is capped.
- **If missing:** Add a mutual cap (e.g. total liability limited to fees paid in
  the prior 12 months) and exclude indirect/consequential damages.

## Indemnification
- **Why it matters:** One side agrees to cover certain losses/claims of the other.
- **Look for:** What triggers it, mutual where appropriate, and whether it's
  subject to the liability cap.
- **Red flags:** One-way indemnity against the user; broad, uncapped indemnity;
  indemnifying the other side for their own negligence.
- **If missing:** Add a balanced indemnity for third-party claims arising from
  each party's breach or IP infringement.

## Confidentiality
- **Why it matters:** Protects sensitive information shared under the agreement.
- **Look for:** What's confidential, permitted uses, standard exclusions, and how
  long the duty lasts.
- **Red flags:** One-way when it should be mutual; no time limit that's workable;
  no carve-out for legally required disclosure.
- **If missing:** Add a mutual confidentiality clause (see `nda.md` for detail).

## Intellectual property ownership
- **Why it matters:** Says who owns what is created, used, or licensed.
- **Look for:** Clear ownership of pre-existing IP and of anything created;
  license grants with scope.
- **Red flags:** The user's IP transferring by default; vague "all IP belongs to
  [other party]"; no license back where the user needs to keep using something.
- **If missing:** Add an IP clause: each party keeps its pre-existing IP; specify
  who owns deliverables and any license grants.

## Warranties & disclaimers
- **Why it matters:** Promises about quality/authority, and what's disclaimed.
- **Look for:** Reasonable, mutual warranties (e.g. authority to enter the
  agreement) and clearly scoped disclaimers.
- **Red flags:** The user giving broad warranties while the other side disclaims
  everything ("as is").
- **If missing:** Add mutual authority warranties and a balanced disclaimer.

## Assignment
- **Why it matters:** Controls whether a party can transfer the contract to
  someone else.
- **Look for:** Consent required to assign, usually with an exception for mergers/
  acquisitions.
- **Red flags:** The other side can freely assign to anyone; the user cannot
  assign even in a sale of its business.
- **If missing:** Add "Neither party may assign without the other's consent,
  except to a successor in a merger or sale of substantially all assets."

## Notices
- **Why it matters:** Says how official communications (like termination) must be
  sent to count.
- **Look for:** Addresses, accepted methods (email/registered post), and when
  notice is deemed received.
- **Red flags:** Only physical mail accepted; addresses missing.
- **If missing:** Add a notices clause with addresses and accepted delivery
  methods including email.

## Entire agreement
- **Why it matters:** States the written contract is the full deal, so side
  promises don't bind.
- **Look for:** A clause saying this document supersedes prior discussions.
- **Red flags:** None if present; its absence can let disputed oral promises in.
- **If missing:** Add "This Agreement is the entire agreement between the parties
  and supersedes all prior discussions."

## Severability
- **Why it matters:** If one clause is invalid, the rest of the contract survives.
- **Look for:** A standard severability clause.
- **If missing:** Add "If any provision is held unenforceable, the remaining
  provisions remain in full force."

## Force majeure
- **Why it matters:** Excuses performance during events outside a party's control.
- **Look for:** A reasonable list of events and a notice/mitigation duty.
- **Red flags:** Only the other side benefits; overly broad excuse that lets them
  avoid paying indefinitely.
- **If missing:** Add a mutual force majeure clause covering events beyond
  reasonable control.

## Amendment
- **Why it matters:** Prevents informal changes to the deal.
- **Look for:** Changes only valid if in writing and signed by both parties.
- **If missing:** Add "This Agreement may only be amended in writing signed by
  both parties."

## Data protection / privacy
- **Why it matters:** Required whenever personal data is shared or processed.
- **Look for:** Who is controller/processor, security obligations, and compliance
  with applicable law (e.g. GDPR).
- **Red flags:** Personal data handled with no security or compliance terms.
- **If missing:** Add a data-protection clause (or a Data Processing Addendum)
  if any personal data is involved.
