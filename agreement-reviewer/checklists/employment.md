# Checklist: Employment / Contractor Agreement

Apply **in addition to** `common-clauses.md`. Clauses specific to employment,
offer letters, and consultant/contractor agreements.

> Note: employment law varies a lot by country/state, and some clauses
> (non-competes especially) may be limited or unenforceable where the person
> works. Flag these for local-law confirmation.

Each entry: **Why it matters** · **Look for** · **Red flags** · **If missing**.

---

## Role, duties & reporting
- **Why it matters:** Defines the job and expectations.
- **Look for:** Job title, key duties, and who the person reports to.
- **Red flags:** Duties defined so broadly the employer can reassign to anything;
  "other duties as assigned" with no limit.
- **If missing:** Add a role description with core responsibilities.

## Employee vs contractor classification
- **Why it matters:** Misclassifying a worker as a contractor creates tax and
  legal liability.
- **Look for:** Consistency between the label and the reality (control, hours,
  tools, exclusivity).
- **Red flags:** "Contractor" who works fixed hours under close control like an
  employee.
- **If missing / risky:** Flag for review; align the terms with the true working
  relationship.

## Compensation & benefits
- **Why it matters:** Pay, bonus, equity, and benefits are the core of the deal.
- **Look for:** Salary/rate, pay frequency, bonus/commission terms, equity
  details (vesting), and benefits.
- **Red flags:** Discretionary bonus with no criteria; equity mentioned but no
  vesting schedule or documents; rate but no payment schedule (contractor).
- **If missing:** Add clear compensation terms and, for equity, the vesting
  schedule and governing plan.

## Working hours, location & expenses
- **Why it matters:** Sets expectations and reimbursement.
- **Look for:** Hours/FTE, location or remote terms, and expense reimbursement.
- **Red flags:** Unlimited availability expected; no expense policy.
- **If missing:** Add hours/location terms and an expense-reimbursement clause.

## At-will status / notice period
- **Why it matters:** How the relationship can end.
- **Look for:** At-will language (US) or a notice period (many other countries);
  applied to both sides.
- **Red flags:** Long notice required from the worker but none from the employer;
  no notice at all where law expects it.
- **If missing:** Add the appropriate termination/notice terms for the
  jurisdiction, balanced for both sides.

## Termination & severance
- **Why it matters:** What happens, and what's paid, on exit.
- **Look for:** Grounds for termination for cause, severance terms, and treatment
  of accrued pay/leave.
- **Red flags:** Broad "for cause" with no definition; forfeiture of earned
  wages; no severance where expected.
- **If missing:** Add a clear "for cause" definition and severance/accrued-pay
  terms.

## IP assignment
- **Why it matters:** Ensures work created belongs to the employer/client.
- **Look for:** Assignment of work-related IP, with carve-outs for prior
  inventions and (where required) statutory exclusions for personal projects.
- **Red flags:** Assignment of **all** IP including unrelated personal projects;
  no carve-out for pre-existing inventions.
- **If missing:** Add an IP-assignment clause covering work product, with a
  prior-inventions carve-out.

## Confidentiality
- **Why it matters:** Protects employer information during and after the role.
- **Look for:** Confidentiality obligations that survive termination.
- **Red flags:** No confidentiality at all; or so broad it blocks the worker's
  ordinary skills/knowledge.
- **If missing:** Add a confidentiality clause surviving the end of employment.

## Non-compete (enforceability varies)
- **Why it matters:** Restricts working for competitors after leaving — heavily
  regulated and often unenforceable.
- **Look for:** Reasonable scope, geography, and duration — and whether it's even
  allowed where the person works.
- **Red flags:** Broad, long, no-geographic-limit non-compete; may be void; treat
  as High severity and flag for local-law review.
- **If missing:** Often fine to omit; if included, ensure it's narrow and lawful.

## Non-solicitation
- **Why it matters:** Restricts poaching clients/employees after leaving — usually
  more enforceable than non-competes.
- **Look for:** Reasonable duration (e.g. 12 months) and clear scope.
- **Red flags:** Indefinite or extremely broad non-solicit.
- **If missing:** Add a reasonable non-solicit if protection is needed.

## Restrictive covenants — overall balance
- **Why it matters:** Stacked restrictions (non-compete + non-solicit +
  confidentiality + IP) can overreach.
- **Look for:** That the bundle is reasonable as a whole and lawful locally.
- **Red flags:** Aggressive, one-sided restrictions with no consideration.
- **If risky:** Recommend narrowing to what's enforceable and necessary.
